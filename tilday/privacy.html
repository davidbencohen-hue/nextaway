Privacy Policy

Tilday for iPhone and iPad · Effective November 27, 2026

The short version

Tilday does not collect, store or sell your personal information. There are no Tilday accounts, no ads and no analytics or tracking. The developer has no server. Your countdowns, and anything read from your calendar or your Gmail, stay on your device and in your own iCloud. The reading and the suggesting are done on the device itself, with Apple Intelligence where your device has it.

What stays on your device

  • Your countdowns and trips. Names, dates, plans and photos you choose are saved on your device. The developer cannot see them.
  • Your calendar. If you allow calendar access, Tilday reads your events on the device so you can turn them into countdowns. Calendar data is not sent anywhere.
  • Screenshots and pasted confirmations. If you give Tilday a screenshot or paste a booking confirmation, the words are read on the device to fill in a flight or a stay.
  • Notifications. When a new trip is found in your Gmail, Tilday may post one quiet notification on this device, at most one a day. It is created on the device; we send nothing to your phone and have no notification server.
  • Your Gmail, if you connect it. See the next section.

Gmail and Google user data

Connecting a Gmail account is optional. If you connect one, this is exactly what happens.

  • What Tilday accesses. You sign in with Google's own sign-in screen and grant Tilday read-only access to your Gmail (the gmail.readonly permission), plus your email address so the app can show which account is connected. Tilday cannot send, change or delete email.
  • How it is used. For a trip, the app on your device searches your mailbox for travel booking confirmations (flights, places to stay, rental cars, trains and tickets) and reads the matching messages to find the booking's details: what it is, its dates and times, the place and the confirmation code. Those details are shown on the trip. That is the only use.
  • Finding new trips. Tilday also looks through your recent travel confirmations for trips that have no countdown yet and offers them at the top of your list. It looks when you open the app and, when iOS allows a background refresh, occasionally while the app is closed. The search and the reading happen on your device. Nothing is added unless you tap Add, and a suggestion you dismiss is not offered again. You can turn background checks off in the app (the ••• menu beside "Gmail checked" on a trip) or in iOS Settings > General > Background App Refresh.
  • Where it is processed. Entirely on your device. Messages travel directly from Google to your device over an encrypted connection and are read in memory by the app, including by Apple's on-device language model where your device has it. Nothing from your Gmail is sent to the developer, because there is no developer server. No email text is sent to any other company or service.
  • Logos and listing pictures. To draw a company's logo on a booking, the app asks an icon service (Google, DuckDuckGo or the company's own website) for it by the company's web address or airline code, for example "delta.com". To show a rental's picture, it loads the picture from the address printed in the booking email. These requests carry no email text, no booking details and nothing that identifies you beyond what any internet request includes (your IP address).
  • What is stored. Email text is not stored. The details of a booking that was found (what it is, its dates, place and confirmation code) and the name and dates of a suggested trip are kept on your device so they can be shown again without searching. A booking you keep is saved with your trip like anything else you add to it, and syncs through your own iCloud with the rest of the trip. Your Google sign-in is kept in the device's Keychain, on that device only.
  • Sharing. Tilday does not share, transfer, sell or disclose Google user data to anyone. No person at the developer can read your email, because it never leaves your device.
  • Never used for. Advertising of any kind, building profiles, or creating, training or improving artificial intelligence or machine learning models.
  • Removing access and deleting data. Choose Disconnect Gmail in the app (the ••• menu beside "Gmail checked" under any trip's plan). That revokes Tilday's access at Google and deletes the saved sign-in from the device. You can also remove access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Bookings you added stay on your trips until you delete them there; deleting the app deletes everything it saved on the device.

Tilday's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What leaves your device

  • Your iCloud. Your countdowns and plans sync between your own devices through your own iCloud account, under Apple's privacy policy. The developer cannot see them.
  • Maps and flights you look up. When you type a place for a stay, the app asks Apple Maps for it. When you type a flight number yourself, the app asks open flight-data services (adsbdb.com and adsb.lol) for its route and position. A flight found in your Gmail is not looked up this way. Links such as Track, Directions and Manage open the airline's, FlightAware's or Apple Maps' own pages.
  • Photo searches. To find a picture for a countdown, the app sends the search words (usually the destination name) to photo services: Unsplash, Pexels, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons. Like any internet request, this includes your IP address. Those services handle the request under their own privacy policies.
  • Voice input. If you tap the microphone, your speech is turned into text by Apple's speech recognition, on the device when your device supports that; otherwise Apple may process the audio on its servers under Apple's privacy policy. Tilday does not keep recordings.
  • Purchases. Tips and the Tilday Supporter subscription are handled by Apple through the App Store. The developer never sees your payment details.
  • Google, if you connect Gmail. Signing in and reading your mailbox are requests from your device to Google, under Google's privacy policy.

Sharing trips

When you share trips, Tilday makes a link that holds each trip's name, dates and plan titles. Notes, photos and booking details such as confirmation codes are not included. The trips are inside the link itself and are not stored on a server; anyone you give the link to can read them.

Children

Tilday does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
